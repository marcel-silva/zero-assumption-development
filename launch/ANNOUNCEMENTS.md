## LinkedIn / developer community post

When you ask AI to add caching to a slow endpoint, you've already picked a solution. You might be right. But have you checked what is actually slowing it down?

I'm putting together Zero-Assumption Development (ZAD), a question-first workflow for working with AI coding assistants.

The starting point is to assume neither you nor the AI understands the task yet. That doesn't mean throwing out your experience. It means treating your first explanation as something to check, rather than something the AI should immediately turn into code.

Every developer prompt is a question. The AI responds normally: it investigates, explains what it finds, and makes changes when the work is clearly authorised. It asks for input when a decision depends on information it cannot establish itself.

The toolkit includes a working agreement, examples, a manifesto, and a small testable demonstration. I haven't established that this is faster or more accurate than other approaches. I would like people to try it on one task and report where it helps and where it gets in the way.

Repository: https://github.com/marcel-silva/zero-assumption-development

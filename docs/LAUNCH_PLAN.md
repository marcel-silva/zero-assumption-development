# Proposed 30-day launch sequence

Day 1 means the actual public release date, not the date this package was prepared. This plan does not schedule any activity. The toolkit can be published in stages; approve the withheld README files before treating the repository as ready for a public launch.

## Days 1–7: publish and make the first trial easy

Publish the repository, check links and loading instructions, and make a release from the reviewed commit. Record a short demonstration on a public or synthetic task. Label any authored walkthrough honestly.

Use the draft announcement in `launch/ANNOUNCEMENTS.md` after verifying the live repository URL. Invite a small number of developers to try one bounded task. Do not seek generic endorsements before they have used it.

## Days 8–14: collect experience

Ask testers to record what they asked, what changed, what was actually verified, and what overhead the process introduced. Prioritise reports where an important assumption was found or where questioning became unnecessary friction.

Triage feedback through the experience-report issue template. Fix confusing onboarding before adding a CLI or more abstractions.

## Days 15–21: demonstrate a fair comparison

Run a small pilot using `evaluation/PROTOCOL.md`. Match context and constraints across conditions. Publish failures, budgets, and limitations. Do not claim the pilot proves general superiority.

## Days 22–30: publish what changed

Summarise the reports, revise the agreement only where evidence supports it, and publish clear release notes. Invite contributors to improve examples, independent grading, or integrations.

## Suggested indicators

Track developers who actually tried ZAD, repeat use after an initial task, reproducible reports, meaningful contributions, and independent demonstrations. Stars and page views can be recorded, but they are not evidence that the methodology improves development.

Initial numeric targets should be treated as planning choices, not adoption forecasts. Avoid inventing testimonials, endorsements, usage numbers, or benchmark results.

## Distribution boundaries

Share only in relevant communities and follow their current self-promotion rules. Identify yourself as the proposer. Do not automate unsolicited messages or ask people to manufacture engagement.
